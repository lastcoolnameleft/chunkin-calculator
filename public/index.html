Station setup (Event)

One-time setup: at each station, sight the other station. Keep the compass level and use the black 0–360° forward-bearing scale. Do not rotate the bezel or subtract readings.

GPS checks are separate measurements and never replace the original compass result. Record launch GPS when surveying, or enter a confirmed launch location when adding a check.

Optional: Satellite Map Overlay (GPS Coordinates)

Enter latitude/longitude for Station A & B (or tap Capture GPS while standing at the station). Optical theodolite/angle measurements remain the authoritative calculation for competition scores; GPS coordinates provide a visual satellite map overlay and sanity check.

Stations A and B need a clear view of the entire target area. One pair covers every trebuchet. At each station, zero an angle-sighting device (large protractor on a stake, or a phone sighting app) toward the other station — that's 0°.

Trebuchets

For each trebuchet, measure its distance from Station A and its angle from the A→B baseline.

Stand at Station A. Aim toward Station B to set 0°, then turn toward the trebuchet. Enter the angle between those two sight lines. Angles into the target area are positive; angles behind the A–B baseline are negative (down to -180°). This is the trebuchet's position angle, not its launch angle.

Log a shot

Stand at Station A. Aim toward Station B to set 0°, then turn toward the impact. Report the angle between the A→B sight line and the A→impact sight line, with the corner at A. Do not measure from the trebuchet or use an absolute compass bearing. Example: a quarter-turn from B toward the impact is 90°.

Stand at Station B. Aim toward Station A to set 0°, then turn toward the impact. Report the angle between the B→A sight line and the B→impact sight line, with the corner at B. Do not measure from the trebuchet or use the A→B direction as your zero. Both impact angles must be greater than 0° and add up to less than 180°.

Sight the impact and enter each compass reading. No subtraction needed. Stay away from magnetic interference.
